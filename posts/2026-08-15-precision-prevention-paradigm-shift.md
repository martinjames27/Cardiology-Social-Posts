## LINKEDIN POST

For thirty years, cardiovascular prevention has operated on a quiet paradox: we use population averages to make individual decisions — and then act surprised when high-risk patients still present with their first MI.

That paradigm is being dismantled. And this week's JACC captures exactly why.

**The inflection point**

In a Leadership Page published this month in JACC, ACC President Roxana Mehran and Eric Topol lay out what is happening across cardiovascular medicine: a fundamental shift from population-based risk assessment to individualized, AI-driven, biology-informed prediction. Not a distant vision. A present convergence.

The evidence has been accumulating on multiple fronts simultaneously:

**New risk tools that ask bigger questions.** The 2026 ACC/AHA Dyslipidemia Guidelines replaced the Pooled Cohort Equations with PREVENT — a model that removes race as a variable, incorporates kidney function, and extends the risk horizon to 30 years. We are no longer asking only "what is this patient's 10-year risk?" We are asking: what is the trajectory of their cardiovascular biology over the next three decades?

**New biology made actionable.** Lipoprotein(a) — a genetically determined atherogenic particle present in elevated form in roughly 20% of the global population and disproportionately in South Asians — is now measurable, risk-stratifying, and approaching treatability with targeted RNA-based therapies. Polygenic risk scores for ASCVD are moving from research tools toward clinical validation. The genome is becoming part of the preventive toolkit.

**New trials proving primary prevention works.** The VESALIUS-CV diabetes subgroup analysis (JAMA, April 2026) demonstrated that evolocumab reduced first major cardiovascular events by 31% (HR 0.69; p=0.009) in patients with type 2 diabetes and no known atherosclerosis — people who had never had a heart attack or stroke, who would not historically have received a PCSK9 inhibitor. Absolute risk reduction: 2.9% over five years. NNT: approximately 34. A mortality signal emerged as well.

We used to wait for the first event. We don't have to anymore.

**New therapeutics that remove access barriers.** The oral PCSK9 inhibitor enlicitide (CORALreef Lipids, NEJM, February 2026) achieved 57% LDL reduction with a safety profile comparable to placebo. An oral PCSK9 inhibitor — prescribed like a statin, dispensed at a pharmacy — could transform lipid management for the millions who cannot access or afford injectable biologics.

**AI that sees what clinicians miss.** Foundation models trained on ECGs, imaging, and multimodal clinical data are beginning to detect subclinical disease and predict cardiovascular events before symptoms or formal diagnoses. The ARISE pragmatic trial showed AI-ECG cut door-to-balloon time by 14 minutes in STEMI. That is the near-term application. The medium-term application — predictive risk stratification from a routine ECG — is already in clinical validation.

**What this means for the patients we are most worried about**

The precision prevention framework changes the calculus for patients who have been systematically underserved by the population-average approach.

South Asian adults in the United States develop hypertension, prediabetes, and atherogenic dyslipidemia significantly earlier than other groups — often by age 40 to 45 — yet our traditional risk calculators, calibrated on predominantly white, non-South Asian cohorts, systematically underestimate their risk. A 48-year-old South Asian man with prediabetes, central adiposity, elevated Lp(a), and a family history of premature MI may score as "intermediate risk" on a population tool while carrying a biological risk profile that warrants aggressive intervention.

PREVENT helps by dropping race as an input variable. But the larger opportunity is tools built on and validated in diverse populations — where the full phenotype of South Asian cardiometabolic risk is captured, not averaged away.

**The counterargument that deserves respect**

The history of cardiovascular medicine is not without overclaimed precision promises. Calcium scoring debates. JUPITER controversy. Polygenic score skeptics who pointed out that clinical utility requires more than statistical association. Precision tools must be validated rigorously, implemented equitably, and made accessible — not another lever that widens disparities between patients who can afford AI-guided genomic care and those who receive population averages.

The equity test for precision prevention is not whether it works for the affluent and well-connected. It is whether it reaches the patients who have been most underserved by the tools it is replacing.

**The practical question for academic cardiologists**

The science is converging. The system is catching up slowly.

| Domain | Where we are | What's coming |
|--------|-------------|---------------|
| Risk tools | PREVENT (2026) | Multimodal AI risk scores |
| Lipid therapy | Oral PCSK9i (enlicitide) in trials | First oral PCSK9i approvals |
| Primary prevention | VESALIUS-CV proof-of-concept | Expanded PCSK9i indications |
| Lp(a) | Measurement, risk stratification | RNA-based targeted therapy |
| AI in prevention | AI-ECG, imaging | Predictive, longitudinal biomarkers |

The question for academic leaders: How do we build the implementation infrastructure — equitable, accessible, evidence-guided — so this revolution does not arrive only for some patients?

What in precision prevention are you most excited about — and most skeptical of? I'd especially like to hear from colleagues working at the intersection of prevention, equity, and implementation.
