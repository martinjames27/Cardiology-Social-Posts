**Full Post:**

For the first time in history, we have a positive randomized trial in nonobstructive hypertrophic cardiomyopathy.

This week at ESC Congress 2026 in Munich — and simultaneously in the New England Journal of Medicine — the ACACIA-HCM trial changed the landscape for half of all HCM patients.

**The gap this fills**

Hypertrophic cardiomyopathy affects roughly 1 in 500 people. We tend to think of it as an obstructive disease — the dynamic LVOT gradient, the systolic anterior motion, the symptomatic relief from septal reduction. But approximately half of all HCM patients have the nonobstructive form. Until now, that group had no approved therapy and no positive phase 3 trial. Aficamten already had FDA approval for obstructive HCM (SEQUOIA-HCM). ACACIA-HCM closes the gap.

**What the trial showed**

| Outcome | Aficamten | Placebo |
|---|---|---|
| Clinical response (≥3 of 5 outcomes) | **53%** | 13% |
| KCCQ-CSS improvement (treatment effect) | **+11.4 pts** (95% CI 9.6–13.2) | — |
| Peak VO₂ improvement (treatment effect) | **+0.64 mL/kg/min** | — |
| NYHA class improvement | Significant | — |
| NT-proBNP | Significant reduction | — |

- **Design:** Phase 3 RCT, 516 adults with symptomatic nHCM, 19 countries, 36 weeks
- **Dose:** Aficamten 5 mg titrated to maximum 20 mg daily vs. placebo
- All 5 prespecified efficacy outcomes favored aficamten (p<0.001 across the board)
- Safety profile consistent with prior aficamten experience

**How to interpret the numbers**

An 11-point improvement in KCCQ-CSS represents a "large" clinically meaningful difference — the minimal clinically important difference is approximately 5 points. Patients felt meaningfully better. The 53% vs. 13% clinical response gap is the most striking finding: 4 in 10 patients on aficamten crossed a threshold of improvement that almost none on placebo reached.

The pVO₂ increment (0.64 mL/kg/min) is modest in absolute terms — less than what we might call a step-change in functional capacity — but statistically robust and directionally consistent with the symptom data.

**Why this matters beyond the numbers**

Nonobstructive HCM is notoriously difficult to treat. These patients often exhaust beta-blockers and calcium channel blockers, get referred for invasive procedures that weren't designed for them, or are simply told their anatomy isn't amenable to septal reduction. "We don't have great options" has been the honest answer for too long.

Aficamten works at the sarcomere level — reducing excessive myosin-actin cross-bridge formation — a mechanism that makes sense regardless of obstruction. ACACIA-HCM validates that mechanism in nHCM. A supplemental FDA NDA is planned for Q4 2026.

**The broader context at ESC 2026**

This was one of 59 trials across 12 Hot Line sessions. Two others worth noting: STAREE showed atorvastatin reduces major CV events by 30% in healthy adults over 70 — but not disability-free survival, complicating the primary prevention calculus in the elderly. And CARDIO-TTRansform showed eplontersen missed its primary endpoint in ATTR-CM, though with an intriguing signal in the monotherapy subgroup. The science is moving fast.

**What I'll be watching**

Cytokinetics plans to file the supplemental NDA by end of 2026. The outcomes trial (hard events, mortality) will take years. But the symptom and functional data are compelling enough — and the unmet need real enough — that this will shift practice.

For patients with nonobstructive HCM who have tried everything and been told to wait: the wait is nearly over.

## OTHER STRONG FINDS TODAY

1. **CARDIO-TTRansform (ESC 2026, August 28):** Eplontersen, an antisense therapy targeting TTR, failed to meet its primary composite endpoint (CV mortality + recurrent CV events) in 1,432 patients with ATTR-CM — the largest ATTR-CM trial to date. A nominally significant hazard ratio of 0.71 was seen in the eplontersen monotherapy subgroup (no background stabilizer), raising important questions about combination therapy strategy in ATTR-CM. *(Source: Ionis Pharmaceuticals press release; news-medical.net)*

2. **STAREE (ESC 2026, August 28):** In nearly 5,000 healthy adults over 70 with no prior CVD, diabetes, or dementia, atorvastatin reduced major CV events by ~30% over roughly 6 years — but did NOT significantly improve the primary endpoint of disability-free survival. Nuanced implications for primary prevention statin guidelines in the elderly. *(Source: HCPLive, ACC.org preview)*

3. **South Asian Cardiometabolic Health Guidelines (ACC/ASPC, 2025–2026):** The American Society for Preventive Cardiology published a comprehensive clinical practice statement on cardiometabolic risk in South Asians — covering nutrition, physical activity, diabetes, hypertension, and dyslipidemia management with South Asian-specific recommendations.
