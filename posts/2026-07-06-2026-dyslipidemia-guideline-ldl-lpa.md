## BEST ANGLE

Eight years ago, the 2018 ACC/AHA guideline moved away from specific LDL targets toward a "risk-based discussion" framework — a decision that generated significant debate at the bedside. The 2026 guideline reverses course: specific LDL goals are back, Lp(a) is now on every patient's checklist at least once, and the PREVENT equations give clinicians a fundamentally better lens for lifetime cardiovascular risk. For physicians caring for South Asians and other populations with high Lp(a) burden and early-onset ASCVD, these changes are not incremental — they are structural.

## LINKEDIN POST

**The 2026 Cholesterol Guidelines Just Changed How We Think About Prevention**

After eight years without quantitative LDL goals, the 2026 ACC/AHA Dyslipidemia Guideline brings them back — and goes further. Here's what's changed, and why it matters for the patients most at risk.

---

**The headline shift: LDL targets are back.**

The 2018 guidelines dropped numeric LDL goals in favor of risk-based discussions. That framework worked in principle but created ambiguity in practice — when is "low enough" low enough? The 2026 guideline answers that directly:

| Risk Category | LDL-C Target |
|---|---|
| Very high risk ASCVD | < 55 mg/dL |
| Established ASCVD (not very high risk) | < 70 mg/dL |
| High risk (primary prevention) | Risk-guided, with enhanced-risk targets |

These align more closely with European and global standards — and give clinicians and patients a number to aim for.

---

**Lp(a) screening is now universal — and that changes everything.**

The 2026 guideline recommends measuring lipoprotein(a) at least once in every patient's lifetime.

This is not a small edit. Lp(a) is largely genetically determined, reaches adult levels in childhood, and is not meaningfully reduced by statins. The thresholds:

- Lp(a) ≥ 125 nmol/L (50 mg/dL) → risk-enhancing factor (~1.4× elevated ASCVD risk)
- Lp(a) ≥ 250 nmol/L (100 mg/dL) → ≥2× elevated risk

Why does this matter particularly for South Asian patients? Elevated Lp(a) tracks with South Asian ancestry at population level, and premature CAD in South Asians often presents without classical risk factors that score-based tools capture well. Universal Lp(a) testing gives clinicians actionable, individualized data — especially for those who look "low risk" on traditional metrics.

---

**Risk assessment upgraded: PREVENT equations replace the Pooled Cohort Equations.**

The 2019 PCE-derived estimates missed important risk dimensions. The PREVENT equations now incorporate:

- 10-year **and** 30-year cardiovascular risk
- Chronic kidney disease and heart failure burden
- Social determinants of health

This is a meaningful equity advance. Populations with disproportionate metabolic and cardiorenal burden — South Asians, Black Americans, patients with CKD — may be reclassified to higher risk under PREVENT, prompting earlier intervention.

---

**The treatment cascade: clearer sequencing.**

The guideline formalizes a stepwise approach:

1. Lifestyle modification + maximally tolerated statin (first-line, always)
2. Add ezetimibe or bempedoic acid (Nexletol)
3. Add PCSK9 inhibitor (evolocumab, alirocumab) — earlier than 2018 guidance
4. Consider inclisiran (siRNA, twice-yearly dosing) for further LDL lowering

Bempedoic acid gets a formal role for statin-intolerant patients. Inclisiran is formally positioned in the cascade. PCSK9 inhibitors move up — especially for very high-risk patients who aren't at LDL target on maximum oral therapy.

This is clinically significant timing: VESALIUS-CV (NEJM) recently demonstrated that evolocumab reduces major cardiovascular events by 25% even in high-risk patients without prior MI or stroke — the first PCSK9 inhibitor proof of concept in primary prevention. The guidelines and the trial evidence are now aligned.

---

**Why this guideline is renamed "Dyslipidemia" — not just "blood cholesterol."**

The scope expansion is deliberate. The 2026 guideline formally addresses:

- Triglyceride-rich remnant particles as independent ASCVD risk factors
- Non-HDL-C as a treatment target alongside LDL-C
- Lipoprotein(a) as a distinct atherogenic lipoprotein
- Hypertriglyceridemia management (including emerging agents like olezarsen)

LDL-C is necessary but not sufficient. This guideline brings the rest of the lipid panel into the conversation in a structured way.

---

**The broader implication for academic medicine and health systems:**

This guideline — endorsed by 11 professional societies — creates immediate quality measure implications. PCSK9 inhibitor access, LDL goal attainment, and Lp(a) testing will likely become traceable metrics. Health systems that invest in population-level lipid management programs now — particularly in high-risk communities — will be positioned to lead on both outcomes and equity.
