## BEST ANGLE

STAREE is the first large, randomized trial to test statins for cardiovascular primary prevention specifically in adults aged 70 and older — a population that has been excluded from or underrepresented in virtually every major lipid outcomes trial. The headline number (30% MACE reduction) is clear and clinically meaningful. But the primary endpoint — disability-free survival, a composite of death, dementia, or persistent physical disability — was not significantly improved. That juxtaposition is the richest teaching moment in preventive cardiology this fall: statins prevent heart attacks in older adults, but whether they preserve the quality and independence of life older adults care most about is a different, harder question. The trial invites clinicians to think carefully about what prevention is *for*, and in whom.

## LINKEDIN POST (Main — ~560 words)

**STAREE. The trial preventive cardiology has needed for 30 years.**

Presented at ESC Congress 2026 and published simultaneously in the *New England Journal of Medicine*, STAREE is the first adequately powered, placebo-controlled RCT to test statin therapy for primary cardiovascular prevention in adults aged 70 years and older — a population systematically excluded from, or dramatically underrepresented in, every landmark lipid outcomes trial before it.

The results are meaningful. And they are more complicated than the 30% headline suggests.

**The trial:**

9,971 community-dwelling Australians aged ≥70 (mean age 74.7, 52% women) with no history of cardiovascular disease, diabetes, or dementia were randomized to atorvastatin 40 mg daily or placebo and followed for a median of 5.9 years. This is a pragmatic, general-practice trial — not a referral-center population.

**The cardiovascular outcome:**

| | Atorvastatin | Placebo |
|---|---|---|
| Major CV events | 6.0% | 8.3% |
| Relative risk reduction | 30% | — |

Major cardiovascular events — cardiovascular death, nonfatal MI, nonfatal stroke, or coronary revascularization — were reduced by 30%. The benefit was driven primarily by nonfatal events. Statistically significant. Clinically real.

That resolves a long-standing debate: statins prevent heart attacks when started after age 70 in a primary prevention setting.

**The primary endpoint tells a different story.**

STAREE's primary endpoint was not MACE. It was *disability-free survival* — a composite of death, dementia, or persistent physical disability. This choice came from what older patients actually tell us they want: not just to avoid a cardiac event, but to maintain their independence and function.

That endpoint was not significantly different between groups (HR 0.94; 95% CI 0.84–1.05).

So: atorvastatin reduced heart attacks in adults over 70. But it did not demonstrably extend the years lived free of death, dementia, or disability.

**What this means for clinical practice:**

This is not a simple yes or no for statins after 70. It is a framework for a richer conversation — one that every clinician who cares for older adults needs to have:

- For a vigorous, motivated 70-year-old with elevated LDL-C and vascular risk factors, the 30% event reduction likely justifies therapy, particularly given generic atorvastatin's low cost.
- For a frail, multimorbid 82-year-old weighing polypharmacy, fall risk from myopathy, or the logistics of daily medication, the null disability-free survival result reframes the conversation entirely.
- The investigators were direct: decisions should account for *"life expectancy, competing risk, treatment burden, and patient priorities."* STAREE underrepresented frail and very elderly patients — extrapolation to those populations requires care.

**A word on what STAREE means for populations at earlier risk — including South Asians:**

South Asian individuals develop atherosclerosis on average nearly a decade earlier than the general population. Conventional risk algorithms underestimate their risk. Non-calcified plaque accumulates aggressively even at zero or low coronary calcium scores. Premature cardiovascular events — in the 40s and 50s — can be devastating.

STAREE's message for those of us focused on premature CAD is, in part, a reminder of urgency: the moment for meaningful primary prevention is not age 70. It is 40, 45, sometimes earlier. The 30% event reduction that STAREE achieved starting statins in the 8th decade of life may be far greater — and translate into preserved function, not just prevented events — when we intervene in the window that matters most.

**The bigger takeaway:**

STAREE invites us to evolve how we measure the success of prevention. Preventing a nonfatal MI is important. Preserving the years that follow — lived without disability, without dependence — is what patients actually want.

Future prevention trials should be designed around patient-defined outcomes from the start. The field has answered whether to treat. Now we must ask: treat for what, for whom, and at what moment in the life course?

*What's your approach to statin initiation in adults over 70? How do you weigh MACE reduction against disability-free survival in shared decision-making with older patients?*

## OTHER STRONG FINDS TODAY

1. **Lp(a)HORIZON — Pelacarsen Fails to Reduce MACE (NEJM, ~September 7–8, 2026):** Pelacarsen, an RNA-targeted antisense oligonucleotide, reduced Lp(a) by ~80% in ~8,000 patients with established ASCVD — but produced no significant reduction in cardiovascular events. The first large Lp(a)-lowering outcomes trial is negative, raising fundamental questions about Lp(a) as a causal therapeutic target versus a risk marker. Compounded by simultaneous announcement that Novo Nordisk halted the HERMES and ATHENA trials of ziltivekimab (IL-6 inhibitor) for futility — a double blow to the novel CV risk-reduction pipeline this week. Critical context: lepodisiran (a competing siRNA agent with twice-yearly dosing) continues in the Phase 3 ACCLAIM-Lp(a) trial with a different mechanism emphasis. Highly relevant for South Asian patients, who carry disproportionately elevated Lp(a). Source: NEJM / ESC press release (escardio.org).

2. **AIR-STEMI Trial — Functional Coronary Angiography in Multivessel STEMI (NEJM, September 10–11, 2026):** ~1,800 patients with STEMI and multivessel disease randomized to angiography-derived FFR vs. conventional angiography for non-culprit revascularization. Functional guidance reduced cardiovascular events (4.6% vs. meaningfully higher) AND contrast-associated AKI (2.2% vs. 4.0%) simultaneously — a rare finding that improves both efficacy and safety. Squarely in the September 8–15 window. Important for catheterization lab practice and cardiac imaging. Published NEJM Vol. 395, No. 10. DOI: 10.1056/NEJMoa2601841. URL: https://www.nejm.org/doi/abs/10.1056/NEJMoa2601841

3. **2026 ESC Heart Failure Guidelines — HFmrEF Eliminated (European Heart Journal, August 2026):** The new ESC HF Guidelines abolish the HFmrEF classification (EF 41–49%), simplifying the taxonomy to HFrEF (EF <50%) and HFpEF (EF ≥50%). "Foundational Medical Therapy" (FMT) replaces "GDMT" — with SGLT2i + MRA now carrying Class I recommendations for HFpEF for the first time. In-hospital SGLT2i initiation for decompensated HF also newly recommended. The most substantial structural change to HF guidelines in years; actively debated in the post-ESC window. Published in *European Heart Journal* (DOI: 10.1093/eurheartj/ehag100). URL: https://academic.oup.com/eurheartj/advance-article/doi/10.1093/eurheartj/ehag100/8766302
