## LINKEDIN POST

The 2026 ACC/AHA Dyslipidemia Guideline doesn't just change the numbers. It changes the philosophy.

The central insight: LDL-cholesterol risk accumulates like smoking exposure — the longer it stays elevated, the greater the lifetime harm. We've spent decades anchored to 10-year ASCVD risk scores. The new guideline asks a different question: **How many years of atherogenic burden has this patient already carried?**

This is the "lower sooner" principle. And it should reshape how we talk to patients long before their first event.

---

**What changed — at a glance:**

| Domain | Prior Approach | 2026 Recommendation |
|---|---|---|
| LDL target: Very high-risk ASCVD | <70 mg/dL | **<55 mg/dL** |
| LDL target: High-risk ASCVD | <70 mg/dL | **<70 mg/dL** (reinforced) |
| LDL: High 10-yr risk primary prevention | Individualized | **<70 mg/dL** if ≥10% PREVENT score |
| Non-HDL goal: High-risk | Variable | **<100 mg/dL** |
| CAC scoring | Class IIa | **Class I** for risk refinement |
| Lp(a) screening | Selected high-risk patients | **Universal — all adults, at least once** |
| CKD ≥stage 3 (ages 40-75) | Risk calculator-guided | **Primary prevention statin regardless of LDL** |
| HIV (ages 40-75) | Risk calculator-guided | **Primary prevention statin regardless of LDL** |
| Reproductive risk markers | Not formally included | **PCOS, adverse pregnancy outcomes, early menopause now included** |
| ApoB measurement | Supplemental | Encouraged for risk refinement beyond LDL |
| PCSK9 inhibitors | Third-line after ezetimibe | **Equal footing with ezetimibe as second-line add-on** |

---

**Why universal Lp(a) screening is the most underappreciated change**

Lp(a) is largely genetically determined. Elevated levels affect roughly 20–25% of the global population — and are disproportionately prevalent in South Asian, African American, and patients with familial hypercholesterolemia. Until now, Lp(a) testing was reserved for patients already flagged as high-risk. The new guideline recommends testing at least once in all adults.

This isn't academic. Elevated Lp(a) changes risk stratification, informs statin intensity, and shapes PCSK9 inhibitor decisions today — ahead of approved Lp(a)-specific therapies that are already in advanced clinical development.

For South Asian patients — who often develop coronary artery disease a decade earlier than white counterparts, and who carry elevated Lp(a) burden — this recommendation is particularly consequential. It's a step toward recognizing biologically distinct risk that population-averaged equations have long underestimated.

---

**Two new populations that matter**

Adults with HIV and those with CKD stage 3 or higher (ages 40–75) now qualify for primary prevention statin therapy regardless of LDL level or PREVENT score. These are patients who have routinely fallen through the gaps of traditional risk calculators. The guideline is catching up to what the biology has told us for years: a diagnosis of HIV or CKD is itself an independent cardiovascular risk factor.

---

**CAC: from "nice to have" to Class I**

Coronary artery calcium scoring has been upgraded from a Class IIa to a Class I recommendation for risk refinement beyond PREVENT scores. For borderline-risk patients, for those hesitant about statins, and for anyone in whom shared decision-making would benefit from personalized data — CAC is now a guideline-endorsed, front-line tool. This is a meaningful validation for the preventive cardiology community.

---

**The statin-plus era is now written into guidelines**

PCSK9 inhibitors — previously positioned after ezetimibe — are now placed on equal footing as second-line options for patients who can't reach their LDL goal on statins alone. Bempedoic acid and inclisiran are formally recognized as evidence-based alternatives. The treatment algorithm is now a true stepped-care model with multiple validated pathways.

---

**The bigger picture**

These guidelines formally retire the idea that cardiovascular prevention begins at age 50. The "LDL-years" concept demands earlier conversations — with young adults, with patients carrying family histories, with populations at elevated baseline lifetime risk.

We already know that ASCVD risk factors appear earlier in South Asian adults in the US — more prevalent by age 45 compared to other racial and ethnic groups. The 2026 guideline gives us the tools and the framework to act on that knowledge, earlier and more precisely.

---

**A question for your practice:**

For every patient you see with borderline LDL, a family history of premature CAD, or unscreened Lp(a) — are you having the conversation about lifetime risk rather than 10-year risk?

The 2026 guidelines say you should be. And now you have Class I recommendations to back it up.
