## LINKEDIN POST

**The 2026 cholesterol guidelines just reshaped primary prevention. Here's what changed — and the harder question behind the numbers.**

Two major analyses published in *JAMA* this week quantified the impact of the 2026 ACC/AHA Dyslipidemia Guideline on U.S. statin eligibility.

The headline: **87.5 million Americans aged 30–79** without known ASCVD now qualify for primary prevention statin therapy. That's **56.6% of that population** — and **21.5 million people who weren't eligible under the 2018 guideline.**

This is a generational shift in how we think about cardiovascular prevention. Let me unpack what's driving it — and why it matters beyond the numbers.

---

**Three simultaneous changes created this expansion:**

| | 2013/2018 Guideline | 2026 Guideline |
|---|---|---|
| **Risk calculator** | Pooled Cohort Equations (PCE) | PREVENT equations |
| **Time horizon** | 10-year risk | 10-year + **30-year** risk |
| **Initiation threshold** | ≥7.5% (initiate), ≥10% (high risk) | 3–5% (consider), 5–10% (should consider) |
| **LDL goals** | Removed (2018) | Restored: <55 mg/dL (very high-risk secondary prevention) |

The newly eligible are largely younger and at lower estimated short-term risk than those previously recommended therapy:
- Mean 10-year ASCVD risk among newly eligible: **3.1%** (vs. 6.1% among previously eligible)
- Largest gains: adults in their **50s (+26.5%)** and **40s (+17.0%)**

---

**Why PREVENT matters — especially for equity**

The PREVENT equations aren't just a technical update. They **remove race as an input variable** — a meaningful departure from equations developed from cohorts that underrepresented minority populations, including South Asians. PREVENT was derived from over 3 million patients in a more contemporary dataset, and it adds kidney function (eGFR), reflecting what we know about the cardiovascular-renal axis.

This doesn't fully solve the representation problem — we still lack large-scale South Asian-specific validation — but it is a step away from race as a proxy and toward biology.

---

**Why the 30-year risk pathway matters**

We've practiced under a 10-year lens for over a decade. But a 42-year-old with a 3.5% 10-year risk isn't "fine" — they're on a trajectory that unfolds over decades of cumulative LDL exposure and arterial burden.

Thinking 30 years out reframes the entire patient conversation. It opens the door to earlier intervention in younger adults, which is where prevention yields the greatest absolute benefit over a lifetime.

For South Asian patients — who present with premature CAD at rates higher than any other ethnic group — this longer horizon isn't just guideline language. It's an overdue clinical recognition.

---

**The Lp(a) recommendation is quietly significant**

The 2026 guideline formally recommends measuring Lp(a) **at least once in every adult**. Not just in high-risk patients. Not just in families with premature disease. Everyone.

This matters because:
- Lp(a) is elevated in approximately 20% of the global population
- It is predominantly genetically determined and causally linked to ASCVD and aortic stenosis
- It is **disproportionately elevated in South Asians** and in families with FH or premature CAD
- It was systematically undertested in routine clinical practice

Universal screening is a step toward equity in risk identification.

---

**And explicit LDL goals are restored**

The 2018 guideline removed treatment targets. The 2026 guideline brings them back. For very-high-risk secondary prevention:

- **LDL-C < 55 mg/dL**
- **Non-HDL-C < 85 mg/dL**

The evidence supported this years ago. The guidelines have caught up.

---

**The harder question**

21.5 million newly eligible patients means 21.5 million conversations that need to happen — most in primary care settings where cardiologists aren't in the room.

Shared decision-making is the right framework. But it assumes functional access to care, health literacy resources, affordable medications, and clinical time. These aren't evenly distributed.

We can write excellent guidelines. The question is whether we build the systems — in clinics, in communities, in underserved zip codes — that can operationalize them.

The guideline is the beginning, not the end.

---

Three changes drove this:
→ PREVENT replaces the Pooled Cohort Equations (removes race as a variable; adds kidney function)
→ 30-year risk assessment added as a treatment pathway alongside 10-year
→ Risk thresholds lowered: consider at 3–5%, should consider at 5–10% 10-year ASCVD risk

What often gets missed in the coverage:

**LDL goals are back.** Very-high-risk secondary prevention target: LDL-C < 55 mg/dL. The evidence has supported this for years.

**Universal Lp(a) testing is now recommended.** At least once for every adult. This matters enormously for South Asians, for FH families, and for the roughly 20% of the population with elevated Lp(a) who don't know it.

The implementation challenge: 21.5 million new statin candidates means 21.5 million conversations — most in primary care, with access and resource constraints that guidelines cannot fix.

The guideline is the map. Building the road is the harder work.
