## BEST ANGLE
Pelacarsen cut Lp(a) by 78% over nearly five years — an unprecedented pharmacologic achievement — yet produced no reduction in major adverse cardiovascular events (HR 1.02, 95% CI 0.90–1.15, P=0.76). For a risk factor that Mendelian randomization has long fingered as causal, and that disproportionately burdens South Asian populations (where ~25% carry Lp(a) >50 mg/dL and face the highest population-attributable ASCVD risk of any ethnic group), this result demands careful, nuanced interpretation rather than reflexive dismissal. The question is not whether Lp(a) matters — it still does — but whether lowering it pharmacologically translates to clinical benefit, and whether different agents, delivered earlier, might succeed where pelacarsen did not.

## LINKEDIN POST

**78% reduction in Lp(a). Zero reduction in heart attacks.**

That is the bottom line from Lp(a) HORIZON — the most anticipated preventive cardiology trial in years, presented at #AHA2026 and simultaneously published in the *New England Journal of Medicine*.

It deserves more than a headline.

---

**What the trial showed**

Pelacarsen, an antisense oligonucleotide designed to suppress hepatic Lp(a) production, was tested in 8,323 patients with established cardiovascular disease and elevated Lp(a). Over a median 4.7 years, it reduced Lp(a) levels by 78.4% — a dramatic pharmacologic effect that exceeded expectations. And yet, the primary composite endpoint of MACE occurred in 12.3% of pelacarsen-treated patients vs. 12.1% on placebo. Hazard ratio: 1.02. P-value: 0.76. Statistically and clinically null.

There was also a safety signal worth noting: anaphylaxis occurred in 19 pelacarsen recipients versus none on placebo, though no fatalities.

---

**Why this matters — and why it doesn't close the book on Lp(a)**

Lp(a) is genetically determined, largely resistant to statins, and has been implicated as a causal driver of ASCVD and aortic stenosis through decades of Mendelian randomization data. So why didn't lowering it work here?

There are several credible explanations:

1. **Trial duration.** Five years may be insufficient to see benefit from Lp(a) reduction. Unlike LDL, where event-curve separation with statins appears within 2–3 years, the structural burden of Lp(a) on established atherosclerotic plaques may require longer timelines — or earlier intervention before irreversible plaque has formed.

2. **Mechanism specificity.** Pelacarsen is an antisense oligonucleotide (ASO). The two Lp(a)-lowering agents still in pivotal trials — olpasiran and lepodisiran — are siRNA-based, with fundamentally different pharmacokinetics and potentially deeper, more sustained suppression. The class is not monolithic.

3. **Patient population.** The trial enrolled patients with established CVD — a high-risk group where atherosclerosis is advanced and plaque burden is already set. The window for Lp(a) reduction to reshape outcomes may be wider in primary prevention or earlier-stage disease.

4. **Lp(a)'s biology is not fully understood.** It likely contributes to cardiovascular risk through both proatherogenic and prothrombotic pathways. How much of its pathogenicity is "undone" by reducing circulating levels, versus how much is structural damage already done, remains an open question.

---

**A particular concern for South Asian patients**

This result carries special urgency for South Asians, in whom ~25% have Lp(a) levels exceeding 50 mg/dL — the threshold associated with significantly elevated ASCVD risk. South Asians carry the highest population-attributable risk from elevated Lp(a) among all ethnic groups globally (PAR ~9.5% for AMI). We have been counseling high-risk South Asian patients about their Lp(a) burden for years, anticipating that targeted therapy was coming. Today, that pathway is more uncertain.

That doesn't mean we stop measuring Lp(a). It means we counsel patients with more honesty about what we do — and don't — know.

---

**What comes next**

Two trials remain in progress and have not yet reported:
- **OCEAN(a)-OUTCOMES** (olpasiran, Amgen)
- **The lepodisiran outcomes trial** (Eli Lilly)

These trials will be decisive. If siRNA-based approaches — with their different mechanism and duration of Lp(a) suppression — also fail to reduce events, we will face a harder conclusion about Lp(a) as a therapeutic target. If they succeed, we will have learned something important about how the target is engaged.

---

**The larger lesson**

Lp(a) HORIZON is science working as it should: a bold hypothesis, a rigorous trial, and an honest answer — even when the answer is not what we hoped. The field should resist two temptations: abandoning Lp(a) research prematurely and explaining away this result with post-hoc rationalization that wasn't prespecified.

The question survives. The uncertainty is real. And the patients who have been told their Lp(a) puts them at risk deserve our continued rigor, honesty, and investment in answering this definitively.

## OTHER STRONG FINDS TODAY

1. **"Discontinuing Statins in Older Adults without Cardiovascular Disease"** (*NEJM*, Nov 7, 2026): A randomized trial found that stopping statins in adults aged ≥70 without established CVD was noninferior to continuation for MACE at 2 years. Major implications for deprescribing conversations in geriatric and primary prevention practice — and potentially for how we frame lifetime statin use.

2. **"Long-Term Survival After Initial Invasive or Conservative Management of Stable Coronary Disease" (ISCHEMIA-EXTEND)** (*JAMA*, Nov 7, 2026): Extended follow-up of the ISCHEMIA trial confirms no long-term survival advantage of PCI or CABG over optimal medical therapy alone in stable coronary artery disease. Reinforces the primacy of medical management and raises ongoing questions about appropriate use criteria for revascularization.

3. **"AI-Identified Reasons for Nonescalation of Lipid-Lowering Therapy for Secondary Prevention"** (*JAMA Cardiology*, Nov 7, 2026): An AI-based analysis presented at AHA 2026 examines why LDL remains above goal in many secondary prevention patients — identifying clinician-documented reasons for not escalating therapy. Strong intersection of healthcare innovation, cardiovascular equity, and lipid management quality improvement.
